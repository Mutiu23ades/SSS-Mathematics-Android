61. Straight Lines

Lesson

A straight line is a line whose direction remains constant. In coordinate geometry, a straight line can be represented by an equation.

The general equation of a straight line is:

y = mx + c

where:

  • m = gradient (slope)
  • c = y-intercept

The gradient of a line through two points (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂) is:

m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁)

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the gradient of the line joining (2,3) and (6,11).

m = (11 - 3)/(6 - 2)

m = 8/4 = 2

Example 2: Find the equation of a line with gradient 3 passing through (2,5).

Using y - y₁ = m(x - x₁):

y - 5 = 3(x - 2)

y - 5 = 3x - 6

y = 3x - 1

Practice Questions

  1. Find the gradient joining (1,2) and (5,10).
  2. Find the equation of a line with gradient 2 passing through (3,7).
  3. State the gradient and y-intercept of y = 4x - 5.

Answers

  1. 2
  2. y = 2x + 1
  3. Gradient = 4, y-intercept = -5

62. Graphs of Linear Equations

Lesson

A linear equation can be represented graphically on a coordinate plane. The graph of a linear equation is a straight line.

For an equation such as y = 2x + 1, choose values of x and calculate the corresponding values of y.

A table of values can then be plotted and the points joined with a straight line.

Worked Examples

Example: Complete a table for y = 2x + 1.

When x = -1:

y = 2(-1) + 1 = -1

When x = 0:

y = 1

When x = 1:

y = 3

When x = 2:

y = 5

Points are therefore (-1,-1), (0,1), (1,3) and (2,5).

Example: Find the x-intercept of y = 3x - 6.

At the x-intercept, y = 0.

0 = 3x - 6

3x = 6

x = 2

Practice Questions

  1. Complete a table for y = x + 3 when x = -2, -1, 0, 1 and 2.
  2. Find the y-intercept of y = 5x + 4.
  3. Find the x-intercept of y = 2x - 8.

Answers

  1. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
  2. 4
  3. x = 4

63. Gradient of a Straight Line

Lesson

The gradient measures the steepness and direction of a straight line.

The formula is:

m = rise/run = (y₂-y₁)/(x₂-x₁)

A positive gradient means the line rises from left to right. A negative gradient means it falls from left to right.

Parallel lines have equal gradients.

For two non-vertical perpendicular lines:

m₁m₂ = -1

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the gradient joining (2,4) and (8,16).

m = (16 - 4)/(8 - 2)

m = 12/6

m = 2

Example 2: Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to a line with gradient 4.

m₁m₂ = -1

4m₂ = -1

m₂ = -1/4

Practice Questions

  1. Find the gradient joining (1,5) and (5,13).
  2. Find the gradient joining (2,8) and (6,4).
  3. Find the gradient perpendicular to 2/3.

Answers

  1. 2
  2. -1
  3. -3/2

64. Distance Between Two Points

Lesson

The distance between two points on a coordinate plane can be found using the distance formula.

For points (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂):

d = √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)²]

The formula comes from Pythagoras' theorem.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the distance between (1,2) and (4,6).

d = √[(4-1)² + (6-2)²]

= √[3² + 4²]

= √25

= 5 units

Example 2: Find the distance between (-2,3) and (4,11).

d = √[(4+2)² + (11-3)²]

= √[6² + 8²]

= √100

= 10 units

Practice Questions

  1. Find the distance between (0,0) and (3,4).
  2. Find the distance between (2,1) and (8,9).
  3. Find the distance between (-1,-2) and (2,2).

Answers

  1. 5 units
  2. 10 units
  3. 5 units

65. Variation

Lesson

Variation describes a relationship between quantities. One quantity may change according to another quantity.

The major types include:

  • Direct variation
  • Inverse variation
  • Joint variation
  • Partial variation

In direct variation, y is proportional to x:

y ∝ x

Therefore:

y = kx

In inverse variation:

y ∝ 1/x

Therefore:

y = k/x

Worked Examples

Example 1: y varies directly as x. If y = 12 when x = 4, find y when x = 10.

y = kx

12 = 4k

k = 3

Therefore y = 3x.

When x = 10:

y = 3(10) = 30

Example 2: y varies inversely as x. If y = 6 when x = 5, find y when x = 15.

y = k/x

6 = k/5

k = 30

When x = 15:

y = 30/15 = 2

Practice Questions

  1. If y varies directly as x and y = 20 when x = 5, find y when x = 8.
  2. If y varies inversely as x and y = 12 when x = 4, find y when x = 6.
  3. State the equation for direct variation.

Answers

  1. 32
  2. 8
  3. y = kx

66. Direct Variation

Lesson

Direct variation occurs when two quantities increase or decrease together in the same ratio.

We write:

y ∝ x

Removing the proportionality sign:

y = kx

The constant k is called the constant of proportionality.

Worked Examples

Example: y varies directly as x. If y = 18 when x = 6, find y when x = 14.

y = kx

18 = 6k

k = 3

Therefore y = 3x.

When x = 14:

y = 3 × 14 = 42

Example: If distance varies directly as time and a distance of 120 km is travelled in 3 hours, find the distance travelled in 5 hours.

d = kt

120 = 3k

k = 40

d = 40(5) = 200 km

Practice Questions

  1. y varies directly as x. If y = 15 when x = 3, find y when x = 9.
  2. p varies directly as q. If p = 28 when q = 7, find p when q = 12.
  3. If distance varies directly as time and speed is 60 km/h, find the distance travelled in 4 hours.

Answers

  1. 45
  2. 48
  3. 240 km

67. Inverse Variation

Lesson

Inverse variation occurs when one quantity increases while another decreases so that their product remains constant.

We write:

y ∝ 1/x

Therefore:

y = k/x

or:

xy = k

Worked Examples

Example: y varies inversely as x. If y = 10 when x = 3, find y when x = 15.

xy = k

10 × 3 = 30

Therefore k = 30.

When x = 15:

15y = 30

y = 2

Example: Six workers complete a job in 20 days. Assuming time varies inversely with the number of workers, how many days will 10 workers take?

workers × days = constant

6 × 20 = 120

10d = 120

d = 12 days

Practice Questions

  1. y varies inversely as x. If y = 8 when x = 5, find y when x = 20.
  2. If 4 workers take 15 days, how many days will 10 workers take?
  3. Write the equation for inverse variation.

Answers

  1. 2
  2. 6 days
  3. y = k/x

68. Joint Variation

Lesson

Joint variation occurs when one quantity varies directly as the product of two or more other quantities.

For example:

z ∝ xy

Therefore:

z = kxy

Worked Examples

Example: z varies jointly as x and y. If z = 24 when x = 2 and y = 3, find z when x = 4 and y = 5.

z = kxy

24 = k(2)(3)

24 = 6k

k = 4

Therefore z = 4xy.

When x = 4 and y = 5:

z = 4 × 4 × 5 = 80

Example: If A varies jointly as b and c, write the equation.

A ∝ bc

Therefore:

A = kbc

Practice Questions

  1. z varies jointly as x and y. If z = 30 when x = 3 and y = 2, find z when x = 5 and y = 4.
  2. If A ∝ bc and A = 24 when b = 2 and c = 3, find the constant k.
  3. Write the equation for z varying jointly as x and y.

Answers

  1. 100
  2. 4
  3. z = kxy

69. Partial Variation

Lesson

Partial variation occurs when one quantity varies directly with another quantity but also has a fixed constant.

The general form is:

y = a + bx

Here, a is the constant part and bx is the variable part.

Worked Examples

Example: y varies partly as x. If y = 8 when x = 2 and y = 18 when x = 5, find the equation connecting y and x.

Let y = a + bx.

For x = 2:

8 = a + 2b

For x = 5:

18 = a + 5b

Subtract:

10 = 3b

b = 10/3

Substitute into the first equation:

8 = a + 20/3

a = 4/3

Therefore:

y = 4/3 + (10/3)x

Example: A taxi charges a fixed fee of ₦500 plus ₦200 per kilometre. Write an equation for the total cost C for x kilometres.

Fixed cost = 500

Variable cost = 200x

C = 500 + 200x

Practice Questions

  1. State the general equation for partial variation.
  2. A taxi charges ₦300 fixed fee and ₦150 per kilometre. Find the cost of 8 km.
  3. y = 5 + 3x. Find y when x = 7.

Answers

  1. y = a + bx
  2. ₦1,500
  3. 26

70. Trigonometry

Lesson

Trigonometry is the study of relationships between angles and sides of triangles.

In a right-angled triangle, the three important sides are:

  • Hypotenuse — opposite the right angle.
  • Opposite — opposite the given angle.
  • Adjacent — next to the given angle, excluding the hypotenuse.

The three basic trigonometric ratios are:

sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse

cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse

tan θ = opposite/adjacent

A useful memory aid is SOH CAH TOA.

Worked Examples

Example 1: A right-angled triangle has opposite side 6 cm and hypotenuse 10 cm. Find sin θ.

sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse

sin θ = 6/10

sin θ = 0.6

Example 2: A right-angled triangle has adjacent side 8 cm and opposite side 6 cm. Find θ.

tan θ = 6/8

tan θ = 0.75

θ = tan⁻¹(0.75)

θ ≈ 36.87°

Practice Questions

  1. A triangle has opposite = 5 cm and hypotenuse = 13 cm. Find sin θ.
  2. If opposite = 12 cm and adjacent = 5 cm, find tan θ.
  3. State the three basic trigonometric ratios.

Answers

  1. 5/13
  2. 12/5 = 2.4
  3. sin θ = O/H, cos θ = A/H, tan θ = O/A
