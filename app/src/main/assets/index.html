81. Histogram

Lesson

A histogram is a graph used to represent continuous grouped data. The class intervals are shown on the horizontal axis and frequency or frequency density is shown on the vertical axis.

Unlike an ordinary bar chart, the bars in a histogram touch one another because the data is continuous.

When class widths are unequal, frequency density is used:

Frequency density = Frequency / Class width

Worked Examples

Example: A class has the following distribution:

Height (cm)Frequency
140–1495
150–1598
160–16912
170–1795

The height intervals are continuous classes. A histogram is suitable for displaying this data.

Example: A class has frequency 20 and class width 5. Find the frequency density.

Frequency density = 20/5

= 4

Practice Questions

  1. What type of data is commonly represented by a histogram?
  2. Find the frequency density when frequency = 30 and class width = 10.
  3. Why do histogram bars touch?

Answers

  1. Continuous grouped data.
  2. 3
  3. Because the data is continuous.

82. Frequency Polygon

Lesson

A frequency polygon is a line graph used to represent a frequency distribution. It is formed by plotting class midpoints against frequencies and joining the points with straight lines.

The midpoint of a class interval is:

Midpoint = (Lower boundary + Upper boundary)/2

Worked Examples

Example: Find the midpoint of the class 10–19.

Midpoint = (10 + 19)/2

= 14.5

Example: For classes 0–9, 10–19 and 20–29, find their midpoints.

0–9: 4.5

10–19: 14.5

20–29: 24.5

These midpoints can be plotted against their frequencies to form a frequency polygon.

Practice Questions

  1. Find the midpoint of 20–29.
  2. Find the midpoint of 30–39.
  3. What is plotted on the horizontal axis of a frequency polygon?

Answers

  1. 24.5
  2. 34.5
  3. Class midpoints.

83. Ogive

Lesson

An ogive is a cumulative frequency graph. It is used to show the total frequency up to a particular value.

There are two common types:

  • Less-than cumulative frequency curve.
  • More-than cumulative frequency curve.

A less-than ogive is plotted using upper class boundaries and cumulative frequencies.

Worked Examples

Example: Frequencies are 5, 7, 8 and 10. Find the less-than cumulative frequencies.

First = 5

Second = 5 + 7 = 12

Third = 12 + 8 = 20

Fourth = 20 + 10 = 30

Example: If the cumulative frequency at a class boundary is 25, what does this mean?

It means that 25 observations are at or below that boundary for a less-than cumulative frequency.

Practice Questions

  1. Find the cumulative frequencies for 4, 6, 5 and 9.
  2. What type of graph is an ogive?
  3. What is plotted on the vertical axis of an ogive?

Answers

  1. 4, 10, 15, 24
  2. Cumulative frequency graph.
  3. Cumulative frequency.

84. Measures of Central Tendency

Lesson

Measures of central tendency are values used to represent the centre or typical value of a set of data.

The three main measures are:

  • Mean
  • Median
  • Mode

The mean is calculated by dividing the sum of all observations by the number of observations.

The median is the middle value after arranging the data in order.

The mode is the value that occurs most frequently.

Worked Examples

Example: Find the mean, median and mode of 2, 3, 3, 5 and 7.

Mean = (2+3+3+5+7)/5 = 20/5 = 4

Median = 3

Mode = 3

Example: Find the median of 4, 9, 2, 7, 6.

Arrange: 2, 4, 6, 7, 9

The middle value is 6.

Practice Questions

  1. State the three measures of central tendency.
  2. Find the mode of 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 5, 8.
  3. Find the median of 3, 8, 1, 6 and 5.

Answers

  1. Mean, median and mode.
  2. 5
  3. 5

85. Mean

Lesson

The mean, commonly called the average, is obtained by dividing the sum of all observations by the number of observations.

Mean = Sum of observations / Number of observations

For a frequency distribution:

Mean = Σfx / Σf

where f is frequency and x is the observation or class midpoint.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the mean of 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12.

Sum = 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 + 12 = 40

Number of values = 5

Mean = 40/5

= 8

Example 2: Find the mean of the following frequency distribution.

xf
23
42
65

Σfx = (2×3) + (4×2) + (6×5)

= 6 + 8 + 30 = 44

Σf = 3 + 2 + 5 = 10

Mean = 44/10 = 4.4

Practice Questions

  2. Find the mean of 2, 4, 6 and 8.
  3. Find the mean of the distribution x = 1,2,3 with frequencies 2,3,5 respectively.

  1. 15
  2. 5
  3. 2.3

86. Median

Lesson

The median is the middle value of an ordered data set.

First arrange the values from smallest to largest.

If there is an odd number of observations, the median is the single middle value.

If there is an even number of observations, the median is the average of the two middle values.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the median of 3, 8, 2, 9 and 5.

Arrange: 2, 3, 5, 8, 9

Middle value = 5

Example 2: Find the median of 2, 4, 7, 9, 11 and 15.

The two middle values are 7 and 9.

Median = (7 + 9)/2

= 8

Practice Questions

  1. Find the median of 4, 7, 1, 9 and 5.
  2. Find the median of 2, 6, 8 and 10.
  3. Find the median of 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 15.

Answers

  1. 5
  2. 7
  3. 8

87. Mode

Lesson

The mode is the value that occurs most frequently in a data set.

A data set can have:

  • No mode
  • One mode
  • Two modes (bimodal)
  • More than two modes

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the mode of 2, 4, 4, 5, 6, 4 and 8.

The number 4 occurs three times, more than any other value.

Mode = 4

Example 2: Find the mode of 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5.

Both 3 and 4 occur twice.

The data is bimodal: modes = 3 and 4.

Practice Questions

  1. Find the mode of 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 2, 5.
  2. Find the mode of 6, 7, 8, 6, 9, 6.
  3. Find the modes of 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5.

Answers

  1. 2
  2. 6
  3. 2 and 3

88. Measures of Dispersion

Lesson

Measures of dispersion describe how spread out the observations in a data set are.

Important measures include:

  • Range
  • Variance
  • Standard deviation

A small measure of dispersion indicates that values are close together. A large measure indicates greater spread.

Worked Examples

Example: Compare the spread of A = 4, 5, 6 and B = 1, 5, 9 using the range.

Range of A = 6 - 4 = 2

Range of B = 9 - 1 = 8

Therefore B has greater spread.

Example: Find the range of 3, 7, 10, 12 and 15.

Range = highest - lowest

= 15 - 3

= 12

Practice Questions

  1. Name three measures of dispersion.
  2. Find the range of 5, 9, 12 and 20.
  3. Which indicates greater spread: a standard deviation of 2 or 8?

Answers

  1. Range, variance and standard deviation.
  2. 15
  3. 8

89. Range

Lesson

The range is the simplest measure of dispersion. It is the difference between the largest and smallest values in a data set.

Range = Maximum value - Minimum value

A larger range generally indicates greater spread in the data.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find the range of 3, 8, 12, 15 and 20.

Maximum = 20

Minimum = 3

Range = 20 - 3

= 17

Example 2: The lowest score in a test is 25 and the highest is 92. Find the range.

Range = 92 - 25

= 67

Practice Questions

  2. Find the range of 12, 15, 20 and 31.

90. Variance

Lesson

Variance measures how far observations are spread around their mean.

For a population:

Variance = Σ(x - mean)² / n

Another useful formula is:

Variance = Σx²/n - (mean)²

The variance is measured in squared units.

Worked Examples

Example: Find the variance of 2, 4 and 6.

Mean = (2+4+6)/3 = 4

Deviations from mean:

2 - 4 = -2

4 - 4 = 0

6 - 4 = 2

Squared deviations:

4, 0, 4

Variance = (4+0+4)/3

= 8/3 ≈ 2.67

Example: If the mean of a data set is 10 and the variance is 9, what is the standard deviation?

Standard deviation = √variance

= √9

= 3

Practice Questions

  1. Find the variance of 1, 2 and 3.
  2. Find the variance of 2, 4 and 6.
  3. If variance is 16, find the standard deviation.

Answers

  1. 2/3 ≈ 0.67
  2. 8/3 ≈ 2.67
  3. 4
