71. Trigonometric Ratios

Lesson

In a right-angled triangle, the three basic trigonometric ratios are sine, cosine and tangent.

sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse

cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse

tan θ = opposite/adjacent

The relationship is remembered using SOH CAH TOA.

Worked Examples

Example 1: A right triangle has opposite side 8 cm and hypotenuse 10 cm. Find θ.

sin θ = 8/10 = 0.8

θ = sin⁻¹(0.8)

θ ≈ 53.13°

Example 2: If adjacent = 12 cm and hypotenuse = 13 cm, find cos θ.

cos θ = 12/13

cos θ ≈ 0.923

Practice Questions

  1. If opposite = 7 cm and hypotenuse = 25 cm, find sin θ.
  2. If opposite = 9 cm and adjacent = 12 cm, find tan θ.
  3. If cos θ = 0.5 and θ is acute, find θ.

Answers

  1. 7/25 = 0.28
  2. 3/4 = 0.75
  3. 60°

73. Trigonometric Identities

Lesson

A trigonometric identity is an equation that is true for all permitted values of the variable.

The fundamental identity is:

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Other important identities are:

tan θ = sin θ/cos θ

1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

Worked Examples

Example 1: If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ.

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

(3/5)² + cos²θ = 1

9/25 + cos²θ = 1

cos²θ = 16/25

cos θ = 4/5

Example 2: If sin θ = 5/13, find tan θ for an acute angle.

Using the 5-12-13 triangle:

cos θ = 12/13

tan θ = (5/13)/(12/13)

tan θ = 5/12

Practice Questions

  1. State the fundamental Pythagorean identity.
  2. If sin θ = 4/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ.
  3. If tan θ = 3/4, find sin θ for an acute angle.

Answers

  1. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  2. 3/5
  3. 3/5

74. Sine Rule

Lesson

The sine rule is used in non-right-angled triangles when sufficient sides and angles are known.

The formula is:

a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C

Here, each lowercase side is opposite the corresponding uppercase angle.

Worked Examples

Example: In triangle ABC, A = 30°, B = 45° and a = 10 cm. Find b.

a/sin A = b/sin B

10/sin 30° = b/sin 45°

b = 10(sin 45°)/(sin 30°)

b = 10(√2/2)/(1/2)

b = 10√2 ≈ 14.14 cm

Example: If a = 8 cm, A = 40° and B = 70°, find b.

b = 8(sin 70°)/(sin 40°)

b ≈ 11.69 cm

Practice Questions

  1. In a triangle, a = 12 cm, A = 30° and B = 45°. Find b.
  2. State the sine rule.
  3. If a = 10 cm, A = 40° and B = 60°, find b.

Answers

  1. 12√2 ≈ 16.97 cm
  2. a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C
  3. ≈ 13.48 cm

75. Cosine Rule

Lesson

The cosine rule is used to find a missing side or angle in a non-right-angled triangle.

To find a side:

a² = b² + c² - 2bc cos A

To find an angle:

cos A = (b² + c² - a²)/(2bc)

Worked Examples

Example 1: Two sides of a triangle are 5 cm and 7 cm and the included angle is 60°. Find the third side.

a² = 5² + 7² - 2(5)(7)cos 60°

= 25 + 49 - 70(1/2)

= 74 - 35

= 39

a = √39 ≈ 6.24 cm

Example 2: A triangle has sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm. Find the angle opposite the 7 cm side.

cos A = (5² + 6² - 7²)/(2×5×6)

= (25 + 36 - 49)/60

= 12/60

= 0.2

A ≈ 78.46°

Practice Questions

  1. Two sides are 6 cm and 8 cm with included angle 60°. Find the third side.
  2. State the cosine rule.
  3. A triangle has sides 5 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. Find the angle opposite the 6 cm side.

Answers

  1. √52 ≈ 7.21 cm
  2. a² = b² + c² - 2bc cos A
  3. ≈ 73.74°

76. Bearings

Lesson

A bearing is a direction measured clockwise from North.

Bearings are written as three-figure angles. For example:

  • East = 090°
  • South = 180°
  • West = 270°
  • North = 000° or 360°

Bearings are commonly used in navigation, surveying and map problems.

Worked Examples

Example 1: A town B is directly east of town A. What is the bearing of B from A?

East is 90° clockwise from North.

Bearing = 090°

Example 2: The bearing of B from A is 120°. Find the bearing of A from B.

Reverse bearing = 120° + 180°

= 300°

Practice Questions

  1. State the bearing of West.
  2. Find the reverse bearing of 070°.
  3. Find the reverse bearing of 250°.
  4. What is the bearing of South?

Answers

  1. 270°
  2. 250°
  3. 070°
  4. 180°

77. Distances

Lesson

Distance problems involve finding the length between two points or places. In coordinate geometry, the distance formula can be used.

For navigation and bearing problems, trigonometry can also be used to calculate distances.

For a right-angled triangle:

Hypotenuse² = Opposite² + Adjacent²

Worked Examples

Example 1: A person walks 6 km east and then 8 km north. Find the straight-line distance from the starting point.

d² = 6² + 8²

d² = 36 + 64 = 100

d = 10 km

Example 2: Two points have coordinates (2,3) and (8,11). Find their distance.

d = √[(8-2)² + (11-3)²]

= √(36 + 64)

= 10 units

Practice Questions

  1. A person walks 5 km east and 12 km north. Find the direct distance.
  2. Find the distance between (1,2) and (4,6).
  3. A right triangle has sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

Answers

  1. 13 km
  2. 5 units
  3. 15 cm

78. Statistics

Lesson

Statistics is the branch of mathematics concerned with collecting, organizing, presenting, analysing and interpreting data.

Data may be:

  • Qualitative — descriptive information.
  • Quantitative — numerical information.

Quantitative data may be discrete or continuous.

Worked Examples

Mean = Sum of values / Number of values

= (4+7+8+6+10)/5

= 35/5

= 7

2, 3, 5, 7, 8

Practice Questions

Lesson

A frequency distribution is a table that shows values or classes together with the number of observations in each.

The frequency of a value tells us how many times that value occurs.

For grouped data, observations are placed into class intervals such as 0–9, 10–19 and 20–29.

Worked Examples

ValueFrequency

32

52

Total frequency = 1 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 8.

Total = 4 + 7 + 9 + 5

= 25
